\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Chiral SU(5): review of the construction}\par
\vspace{3pt}
25 November 2026
\end{center}
\vspace{-4pt}

\textbf{The proof passes the formal checks described below.}
The unresolved issues concern the physical meaning of its definitions and
the reproducibility of the exported proof files.

\textbf{What is claimed.}
The submission claims a four-dimensional continuum SU(5) gauge theory with
three generations of left-handed fermions in
$\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf5}$, at arbitrarily weak reference coupling.
Its gauge-invariant observables satisfy reflection positivity on a specified
class of positive-time tests, locality, positive energy, Poincar\'e covariance
and stress-tensor Ward identities. The vacuum is cyclic and unique up to a
scalar among translation-invariant states. These properties refer to the same
sequence of lattice theories and the same vacuum.

Three-current correlations provide the proposed test of handedness. A
first-generation matter current and a gauge clover observable have a nonzero
connected correlation at spacelike-separated compact supports. A fitted
inverse squared coupling has normalized slopes, averaged over increasingly
long ranges of UV scales, tending to $43/3$: a gauge contribution
$55/3$ minus fermion screening $4$. Convergence is uniform in volume and
cutoff once the range fits below the cutoff and starts sufficiently far
into the UV.

\textbf{What was checked.}
All 16,523 project Lean files compiled on macOS with Lean 4.32.0 and the
specified versions of all nine dependencies. Source and build-configuration hashes
match the archive. The proof uses only the permitted foundational axioms;
no unproved placeholders enter the solution.

On Linux with Lean 4.35.0, Comparator matched the definitions and final theorem
to the reference specification. Lean's own proof checker and con-ron
(verified mode; 222,008 declarations) accepted the exported proofs. Seven
controls for valid and invalid proofs, checker failure and sandbox containment
passed. Final source, export and tool hashes were verified.

An independent calculation of the representation weights gives
$T(\mathbf{10})=3/2$, $T(\overline{\mathbf5})=1/2$ and cancellation of the
cubic gauge anomaly. The
\href{https://www-hep.phys.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~yujitach/tmp/dummies/dummiessu8.html}{usual one-loop formula}
therefore gives $b_0=55/3-4=43/3$. This checks the group factors; the
construction's renormalization-group argument has not been independently verified.

\textbf{Open questions.}
\begin{list}{\textbullet}{\setlength{\leftmargin}{12pt}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{2pt}}
\item \textbf{Identification of the theory.}
The relation between the formal lattice and continuum fields, gauge invariance
of the chiral measure, and exclusion of unwanted mirror fermions still need
examination.
\item \textbf{Reflection positivity.}
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20084v1#S3.SS2}{Lu and Shao (arXiv:2611.20084, \S3.2)}
find an obstruction for the full free overlap Weyl-field algebra at finite
lattice spacing in two and four dimensions, under their locality and symmetry
conditions. Here, positivity passes from an auxiliary functional to limiting
expectations; the relevance of their result is discussed in Appendix A.
\item \textbf{Evidence for interactions.}
A nonzero connected correlation rules out factorization for the chosen
observables, but composite operators can have connected correlations in a
free theory. The proposed test needs comparison with the free gauge--fermion
theory using the same operator definitions.
\item \textbf{Meaning of the running coupling.}
The normalization explicitly inserts the pure-gauge coefficient $55/3$.
The fitted parameter must still be related to a physically defined coupling.
An average over long scale intervals does not determine a pointwise beta
function, its two-loop coefficient or all-orders perturbative matching.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility.}
The full source build was done on macOS; Linux checks used fresh proof exports
from that build. Reference and solution export hashes differ from the supplied
record despite matching file sizes. The cause is unknown. The successful checks
apply to our recorded exports.
\end{list}

The theorem does not establish a mass gap, confinement, a particle spectrum
or uniqueness of the continuum limit.

\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Appendix A: reflection positivity}\par
\vspace{3pt}
Where positivity enters the construction
\end{center}
\vspace{-4pt}

\textbf{The argument obtains reflection positivity for the specified observables
after a UV limit.}
At finite cutoff, the positivity statement applies to an auxiliary expectation
functional. It does not establish positivity on the full Weyl-field algebra
of the original lattice theory at every nonzero lattice spacing.

For a positive-time observable $F$, the reflection-positivity condition is
$\langle(\Theta F)F\rangle\geq0$, where $\Theta$ includes Euclidean time
reflection and adjoint. Applied to all linear combinations of a finite family
$F_1,\ldots,F_n$, it says that the matrix
$M_{ab}=\langle(\Theta F_a)F_b\rangle$ is positive semidefinite.
This supplies the nonnegative inner product used in
Osterwalder--Schrader reconstruction. The allowed observable algebra
determines the scope of that reconstruction.

\textbf{At finite cutoff.}
The proof distinguishes the original lattice expectations from an auxiliary
functional built from different regulator data. Exact positivity is proved
for the latter, on even observables (even fermion parity) with support in a
band separated from the reflection plane by a prescribed buffer. The
statement requires the cutoff and support to satisfy the construction's
admissibility conditions.

\textbf{Removing the cutoff.}
For each allowed test, the difference between the original and auxiliary
expectations tends to zero as the UV cutoff is removed. In schematic notation,
at fixed volume and probe resolution,
\[
 B_k^{\rm aux}(F,F)\geq0,\qquad
 \bigl|B_k(F,F)-B_k^{\rm aux}(F,F)\bigr|
 \xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}0,
 \qquad B_k(F,F)=\langle(\Theta F)F\rangle_k.
\]
Here $k$ increases as the cutoff is removed. The estimate holds eventually in $k$, subject
to a volume-dependent admissibility threshold. The proof selects convergent
subsequences on which the two functionals have the same limit. That limit is
nonnegative. The estimate alone does not require the original finite-cutoff
expectation $B_k(F,F)$ to be nonnegative.

\textbf{The order of limits and reconstruction.}
Positivity is first passed through the UV limit and then through the volume
limit. There is already a positivity statement at fixed volume after cutoff
removal, provided the support fits the buffered band on the torus. Thus
positivity does not have to wait until the final infinite-volume
reconstruction, but the intermediate result has already taken a UV limit.

The resulting positive forms enter the Hilbert-space construction. The final
theorem identifies the selected state's reflection matrices with Gram matrices
of vectors in that Hilbert space. Its domain consists of finite families of
ordered products of observables supported in positive-time slabs. The final
positivity clause is thus tied to the limiting correlations.

\textbf{Relation to the Lu--Shao result.}
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20084v1#S3.SS2}{Lu and Shao}
show that the full free overlap Weyl-field algebra fails reflection positivity
at finite spacing under their stated locality and symmetry assumptions.
Restricted observables can remain positive, and their result does not exclude
positivity in the continuum limit. It therefore neither directly contradicts
the argument above nor refutes Craig's gauge-invariant measure theorem.

The remaining review must identify the expectations and reflection operation
with those of the intended chiral gauge theory, and determine whether the
allowed observable algebra retains its claimed physical content. These
identifications have not been independently checked.
